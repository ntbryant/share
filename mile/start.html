The big idea

The whole secret

  1. Run three times a week, every week.
  2. Most runs should be slow enough to talk the whole time.
  3. Once a week, run some short fast bits with walks in between.

That's it. Slow running builds your engine. Fast bits teach your legs to move quickly. Showing up every week is what makes both work.

Bonus: 10 minutes of leg strength, twice a week

After a run or on a rest day: 15 squats, 10 lunges each leg, 15 calf raises each leg, a 30-second plank. Do it twice through. Stronger legs make running feel easier and keep knees and shins happy.

Now to race day

Your plan

Four stages. Each one adds a little. The dates fill in from your race day.

  1. Stage 1 · Build the habitNov 16 – Dec 13
    • Fast bits: 6 × 30 sec, building to 8 × 30 sec. Walk 90 sec after each.
    • Chatty run: 20 → 25 min.
    • Longer chatty run: 30 → 35 min.
  2. Stage 2 · Get strongerDec 14 – Jan 10
    • Fast bits: 6 × 45 sec, building to 5 × 60 sec. Walk 2 min after each.
    • Chatty run: 25 → 30 min.
    • Longer chatty run: 35 → 40 min.

    Checkpoint at the end: rest the day before, warm up with a 10-min jog, then run a timed mile with even laps. Aim for 7:23, about 1:50 a lap. Then put your new time in at the top of this page.

  3. Stage 3 · Get race-readyJan 11 – Feb 14
    • Fast bits become fast laps: 4 → 6 × one lap (400 m) at 1:44. Walk or jog 2–3 min between.
    • Chatty run: 30 min, then 4 smooth 20-sec speed-ups (runners call these strides).
    • Longer chatty run: 40 → 45 min.

    Ready test: 4 laps at 1:44 each with only 1 minute rest between. If you can do that, you're ready for 7:00.

  4. Stage 4 · Rest up and raceFeb 15 – Feb 27
    • Keep running 3 times a week, but make each run about a third shorter.
    • Early in race week: 3–4 fast laps at goal pace. Rest the day before the race.
    • Race day: even laps. Lap 1 should feel too easy. Lap 3 is the hard one. Kick from 200 m out.

Stay healthy

Four rules

Slow days are slow

If you can't talk, slow down. Going hard every day makes you slower, not faster.

No hard days in a row

Your body gets faster while it rests. Fast bits need a slow day or rest day after.

Sleep and eat

Aim for 8–10 hours of sleep. Eat a real meal or snack after running. Growing plus running takes a lot of fuel.

Sharp pain means stop

Sore muscles are normal. Pain in one spot, or pain that gets worse as you run, is not. Stop and tell a parent or coach.
